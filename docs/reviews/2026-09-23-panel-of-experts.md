# Panel of Experts — Is the wedding wardrobe planner intuitive, well designed, and can guests review clothes without leaving the page?

Convened 2026-09-23 with the `panel-of-experts` skill against the production build at commit `3aeb659`, running locally with seed data and two sample guests. Six experts, two rounds, orchestrator synthesis. Assessment only: nothing in the code was changed by this review.

- **Head of Merchandising, Indian occasion wear** — world-class at how people choose ethnic wear online and in store, sizing across Indian brands, per-event needs
- **Destination Wedding Planner** — world-class at guest logistics for Rajasthan weddings with large NRI and foreign guest lists, bulk store reservations
- **Staff Frontend Engineer & Accessibility Specialist** — world-class at Next.js App Router, WCAG 2.2 AA, progressive enhancement
- **Principal Security & Privacy Engineer** — world-class at threat-modelling small consumer apps holding personal data, GDPR/DPDP
- **Behavioural Designer / Conversion Specialist** — world-class at form abandonment, physical-action gates, the come-back problem

## Technique applied
Plain multi-round dialectic, two rounds, with round 2 forced onto three questions (catalog model, planner structure, must-fix order). Reason: the decision spans six domains with predictable disagreement between merchandising (guests choose) and logistics (couple buys), and the round-1 admin-leak finding needed independent confirmation before it could anchor the ranking.

## Director of Product Design

**Take.** Intuitive: mostly yes up to the point of submitting sizes. The phone fold on `/` answers "what is this, what do I do first" in one screen: eyebrow, headline, one paragraph, "Enter my sizes". The register form is clean and well-hinted. UI/UX: good visual system, but the product falls over at exactly the moment it matters, the planner (`/me/<token>`). It is 6,360 px tall on a Pixel 7 for a womenswear guest (7.6 screens) and 8,906 px for menswear (10.6 screens): five near-identical forms, five "Save X" buttons, five status radio groups, and the same three womenswear cards repeated verbatim under Sangeet, Pheras and Reception. Reviewing clothes in-page: no. Look cards are category descriptions ("Ethnic wear: anarkalis, skirt sets, sarees, dupattas") with a checkbox; 10 of the 14 seed looks read "Price band not checked", contradicting the home page promise of "real price bands". A guest cannot form a preference from these cards; they are a list of store departments, not outfits, and ticking one is a weak signal for the couple. Other: the private link is the only identity, and the site does nothing to help a phone user keep it.

**Strongest argument.** The planner asks the guest to make five decisions and shows nothing that would let them decide. The card body is store name, garment class, "Price band not checked", a sentence, and "Browse at Fabindia ↗" which leaves the site to a category page. The checkbox is visually detached, so it is unclear what "ticking" means: "I like this category"? "Reserve this"? Then a second question, "What should the couple do?", is asked per event even though the answer is almost always the same across events. Change the model: one status question at the top of the planner ("How do you want to handle this? Reserve for me / I'll shop in Mumbai / Still deciding"), and per event a single compact chooser (radio, not checkbox, or an explicit "tick up to two") with the card reduced to title, store, price or "price unknown", one line of why. Save once for the whole page, not five times. Cards need an image before they can be "chosen"; until product images are allowed, the honest UI is "which store do you want to look at for this event", and copy should say so.

**Biggest counter-risk.** The couple's real workflow may be served fine by the current design: they need sizes, a rough intent per event, and a note; guests will tell them the rest on WhatsApp. If most guests are close friends who already know what a sherwani is, category-level cards plus "Please reserve or pre-order this for me" may be enough signal, and a collapsed, image-led chooser risks over-engineering a site with maybe 60 users. Long pages also complete surprisingly well when each section is self-contained. The 5x save is ugly but not fatal.

**First concrete step.** `src/app/me/[token]/page.tsx`: make the five event forms collapsible and one-open-at-a-time (a `<details>` per event, summary = event name, date, status chip, and "N options"; the first unsaved event open by default), move `event.notes` and `palette` inside the details body, and after `?saved=` redirect, show the "Saved" confirmation in the card header next to the chip rather than beside the submit button (today the confirmation sits ~1,300 px below where the hash lands, so the guest sees no "Saved" at all). One file, no schema change, cuts the page to roughly a fifth on phone.

**Other findings**
- Welcome banner says "Bookmark this page" with no copy-link button, no mailto, no email sent, and no "find my link" flow; on a phone this link will be lost, and the 404 tells them to "Register again", creating duplicate guests.
- Header nav "Your sizes" points to `/register` even for a registered guest; there is no "My planner" entry, so the natural way back leads to a second registration.
- Empty-form errors show raw zod text "Too small: expected number to be >0" on chest/waist/hip; `src/lib/guestSchema.ts` needs friendly messages.
- Headline "every night of Shanai & Rhea's wedding" contradicts the two daytime events listed below it; "every event" is truer.
- "Country you live in" is unmarked yet optional, "I dress from" is odd copy (use "I'll be wearing: Menswear / Womenswear"), and "When do you land in India?" is a free-text field that invites unparseable input.
- "Price band not checked" is shown to guests as if it were their problem; render "Price to be confirmed" or hide, and drop "real price bands" from the home page until coverage is better.
- The Mojaris look (empty `eventSlugs`) is injected into all five menswear events; footwear belongs once, near the profile.
- Events are fully described twice (home and planner); on the planner the dress code, palette and notes push the actual choice ~700 px below each event heading.

## Head of Merchandising, Indian occasion wear

**Take.** Intuitive: yes, the flow (measure → size → tick per event → tell the couple) is clear. UI/UX: clean, but it is a form, not a shop. Question 3: no. A guest cannot evaluate an outfit on this page. The look cards show a garment noun, a store name, and for 4 of 14 looks a price; 10 say "Price band not checked". There is no image, no colour, no fabric, no silhouette. Nobody has ever chosen a lehenga from the words "Lehenga, saree or anarkali · KALKI Fashion · Price band not checked; wide range". Every outbound link goes to a category hub (I fetched `/c/sherwani-for-men`: an SEO landing page with no products) or a client-rendered blank (`/clothing/women-ethnic-wear`), so the guest lands somewhere worse than the card. The data model confirms this: `looks` in `src/db/schema.ts` has no image, colour, fabric or product-URL field, so the couple cannot fix it from `/admin/looks` either.

**Strongest argument.** The womenswear side is not merchandised at all. Priya's Sangeet, Pheras and Reception each show the same three cards: Fabindia "Ethnic wear…", Anita Dongre "Designer lehenga, saree or gown", KALKI "Lehenga, saree or anarkali". Three events, identical options, zero prices, two of three linking to a homepage. For Mehendi and Haldi she gets one card. A woman flying in for a Rajasthan wedding is making the most expensive and least reversible decisions on this site, and the page gives her less than it gives Arjun (8 looks, 2 priced). The fix is not images, which the owner has ruled out; it is per-event, per-price-tier, colour-aware curation: for each event, 3–5 named looks with price band (verified or marked "confirm"), fabric, a colour that fits that event's palette and stays off the couple's reserved red/white, and what it needs (blouse stitching lead time, petticoat, dupatta, footwear). Copy in `src/data/seed.ts` SEED_LOOKS should read like a stylist's shortlist, not a category index.

**Biggest counter-risk.** The couple is the buyer, not the guest. The site's real job may be to collect sizes and a status ("reserve for me") so Shanai & Rhea can walk into Tasva with a list. Read that way, the honest "price band not checked" and the "Between sizes — try both" chip are correct behaviour, and richer cards would create false precision the couple would then have to unwind in store. If the plan is for the couple to shop on the guests' behalf, the site is nearly adequate; my critique assumes guests are expected to decide, which is what "Tick the options you like" implies.

**First concrete step.** Fill the price bands. In `src/data/seed.ts`, 10 looks carry `priceNote: "Price band not checked"`; set `priceFromInr`/`priceToInr` for Tasva sherwani, achkan, bandhgala, indo-western, kurta-pyjama, bundi and mojaris, and for the three womenswear designer looks, each with a row in `docs/SOURCES.md`. Where the brand site cannot be read, use the marketplace band and mark it "confirm". Same day, in `src/app/me/[token]/page.tsx`, render a missing band as a visible "Price to confirm" chip rather than free text. Verification note: Tasva's `/pages/size` still 404s; Myntra's Tasva sherwani listing returned an error page, so I could not verify a sherwani band; the ₹4,900–₹14,900 figure that surfaced in search is unverified.

**Other findings**
- Tasva sizing card is a dead end: seven labels and "bring your measurements" for a guest abroad who cannot try on; the achkan garment-chest snippet in SOURCES is a garment measurement, not to-fit, so not computing is correct, but the card should say Tasva's free alterations happen *after* purchase, which only helps if the couple buys before the guest lands.
- Fabindia women's "tops" chart is being applied to sarees/blouses via "Sarees are one-size; blouses follow the tops chart"; ready blouses rarely follow a kurta chart, and no SOURCES row backs that sentence.
- No dupatta, blouse, petticoat, jewellery, footwear (women), safa/pagdi or shawl guidance anywhere; the Sangeet note says "bring a shawl" but no look offers one.
- Event notes push kurta-bundi sets to Haldi despite "nothing you would mind getting turmeric on"; Haldi should map to the cheapest cotton kurta only.
- No colour coordination with the couple: "avoid red and white" appears once, at Pheras; the palette lines are generic and no look carries a colour.
- Kids and plus-ones have no path except free text.
- Price context for foreign guests is absent: INR only per project rule, but a one-line "≈ USD/GBP at today's rate" and a per-event budget guide (₹2k / ₹8k / ₹25k tiers) would let them pre-decide.
- Duplicated cards across events (Priya sees the same three cards three times) make the page read as thin; per-event de-duplication or "also suits: Pheras, Reception" would halve the scroll.

## Destination Wedding Planner

**Take.** Intuitive for a guest, yes: the register form fits one phone screen, the size card ("L, between sizes, try both") is exactly what a guest needs, and the outbound links are honest. But as a tool for placing bulk reservations in Mumbai it is a guest register, not a buying list. The admin shows who registered; it does not tell the couple what to walk into Tasva Lower Parel and ask for. In-page product review is not needed for this wedding; what is missing is the couple-side pick list, a deadline, and three guest questions. I also found an unauthenticated `curl /admin` returns a 307 whose body still contains guest emails: guest data leaks without the passcode.

**Strongest argument.** The "Please reserve or pre-order this for me" status cannot be acted on. I registered a test guest, ticked "Please reserve" for Haldi with no look selected, and it saved (`savePlan` never checks `lookIds` against status). In `/admin` that row reads "Haldi: Please reserve…" and nothing else. Even when a look is chosen, the look is a category, so a store cannot hold anything against it. The per-event cards' "Sizes: Fabindia L (W) ×1" line aggregates every status, not reserve requests, and is not per store. The CSV has `computed_sizes` as one text blob and `chosen_looks` pipe-joined with URLs, so building a store × garment × size sheet means hand parsing. Change: make "Please reserve" require at least one look and a budget ceiling, and give the admin a reservation view grouped by store, then garment, then size, with guest name, arrival date and phone.

**Biggest counter-risk.** Precision here may be false precision. For 150–400 guests the couple will not reserve a lehenga from a category link no matter how well the list is grouped; in practice they buy a batch of Fabindia kurta sets in S/M/L/XL for the daytime events and let guests self-serve the evening looks. Under that model the current summary counts by Fabindia size are already most of what they need, and the missing piece is the guest-side message "we will buy daytime kurtas for you; evening wear is yours to buy," not more admin structure. I still think the buy-list view is right, because even the batch model needs it split by store and by pick-up (Mumbai vs carried to Rajasthan).

**First concrete step.** Add `src/app/admin/(protected)/reservations/page.tsx` (and a nav link): filter plans to `status === "preorder_for_me"`, join looks and stores, group by store → look title → computed size for that store, and list guest, arrival date, phone, event notes under each group, with a flagged "no look chosen" bucket. Add `store` and `size_at_store` columns to the CSV. One day of work; no schema change.

**Other findings**
- Unauthenticated `/admin` response body contains guest names, emails and measurements; move the `isAdmin()` check into the page components or `proxy.ts`, not only the layout.
- No deadlines or reminders anywhere: `events` has no "respond by" field, and nothing tells a guest that Mumbai buying plus alterations needs answers 6–8 weeks before 10 March; add a `respondBy` date to events and a banner on the planner.
- Form asks "When do you land in India?" but not where: guests flying into Jaipur/Udaipur/Jodhpur never pass a Mumbai store; add "Will you be in Mumbai before the wedding, and for how many days?" (drives who gets carried outfits vs a store list).
- Plus-ones and children exist only as free text; the spec's own `household` lever should become a real field.
- Budget is never captured as a number; "reserve for me" implies the couple spends the guest's money, so a per-guest ceiling and an explicit "I authorise you to buy up to ₹X" line are needed.
- No "not yet responded" list: the admin only knows who registered; add an invitee list or a CSV import to compare against.
- The planner is very tall on a Pixel 7 with five separate Save buttons; a shortcut "Same plan for all daytime events" or one Save for the page would raise completion.
- Content is sourced from Western guides (SOURCES.md cites davidsbridal, theknot); confirm with the couple whether "avoid red" at the Pheras applies, add blouse-stitching lead time (2–3 weeks) to the women's Sangeet/Pheras notes, and consider a welcome dinner/Baraat line if the schedule has one.

## Staff Frontend Engineer & Accessibility Specialist

**Take.** The site is intuitive and coherent at every width I tested (Pixel 7, 1280px, and 320px: no horizontal overflow), the whole flow works with JavaScript disabled (register, validation errors re-rendered, plan saved, edit page pre-filled), bad tokens return a true 404, and the admin group returns 307 to login without the cookie and `/admin/export` returns 401. [Orchestrator note: the 307 body still contains guest data; see settled fact above.] Question 3 is a no: nothing on the planner shows a garment, and the outbound "Browse at ↗" link is nested inside the checkbox label. Where it falls short is WCAG AA on the forms: the register page fails focus visibility, grouping, error association and page titling, and the destructive actions have no confirmation.

**Strongest argument.** `src/components/GuestForm.tsx` lines 52–76 and 83–93: the "I dress from" and "cm / inches" choices are radios with `className="sr-only"` inside labels, with no `fieldset`/`legend` and no `has-[:focus-visible]` styling on the label. `<label for="wardrobe">` points at nothing, so the group has no name; tabbing to the radio produces no visible indicator at all, a 2.4.7 failure on the one control every guest must set. Errors compound it: after a failed submit, the error text appears but focus stays on `body`, there is no `aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby` or live region, the hint text is replaced rather than kept, and the range check in `guestSchema.ts` only runs after the zod pass, so "chest 400" was accepted silently on the first round and only rejected on the second. Empty numbers produce zod's raw "Too small: expected number to be >0". Fix: `fieldset`/`legend` for both groups, `has-[:focus-visible]:ring-2` on the card labels, `aria-describedby` pointing at hint+error, `aria-invalid`, a `role="alert"` summary that receives focus, and human messages.

**Biggest counter-risk.** The audience is a few hundred wedding guests on phones with a mouse-free tap flow; the sr-only radios are perfectly usable by touch, the fields have a clear saffron focus ring, and every hint and chip passes contrast (ink-soft on paper 6.53:1, on paper-deep chips 5.90:1). One could argue the a11y fixes are polish that shouldn't delay the product-image question. I still rank them first because they are an afternoon's work and a keyboard or screen-reader guest currently cannot tell which wardrobe they picked.

**First concrete step.** In `src/components/GuestForm.tsx`, replace the two radio blocks with `<fieldset><legend className="label">…</legend>` and add `has-[:focus-visible]:ring-2 has-[:focus-visible]:ring-saffron` to the label classes; delete the orphan `htmlFor` for the wardrobe group. Same day, add `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` in `Field` and set messages on `num` in `guestSchema.ts`. Ships in a day, no schema change.

**Other findings**
- `globals.css`: `btn-primary` white on saffron `#c9741d` is 3.50:1 at 14px semibold, below 4.5:1; use `saffron-deep` (`#9e5710`, 5.49:1) as the resting colour.
- No page sets its own `<title>`; export `metadata` per `page.tsx`.
- "Delete everything about me" and admin "Remove" are one-click, irreversible, unconfirmed (3.3.4).
- Admin events/looks forms: every `<label>` lacks `htmlFor`; measured 42/42 and 150/240 unlabelled controls.
- The `<a target="_blank">` sits inside the checkbox `<label>`, giving the checkbox a 191-character accessible name and a tab stop inside a label; move the link outside and add "(opens in new tab)".
- `src/lib/csv.ts` never neutralises cells starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`; a guest name like `=HYPERLINK(...)` becomes a formula in Excel.
- Scale: admin renders in 634 ms for 6 guests, but the guests table has no search or pagination and at 1280px the last columns are already clipped; at 300 rows ~1 MB of HTML (extrapolated).
- `adminAuth.ts`: outside Vercel, production falls back to the hard-coded `shanai-rhea` passcode; make `ADMIN_PASSCODE` mandatory whenever `NODE_ENV === "production"`.

## Principal Security & Privacy Engineer

**Take.** For a private wedding site this is mostly sound: 144-bit guest tokens, HttpOnly/SameSite=Lax signed admin cookie, server-side zod validation, React escaping (an `<img onerror>` note rendered as text), `rel="noreferrer"` on store links (verified: Referer to tasva.com was null and `window.opener` null), and CSRF is covered (cross-site POST sent no cookie and bounced; same cookie with a foreign `Origin` got Next's "invalid action" and the row survived). `/admin/export` without a cookie is 401; a tampered cookie is 401. One real hole spoils it: the admin guest table is served to anyone who asks. Fix that and the rest is hygiene.

**Strongest argument.** High (must-fix before any guest link goes out). Auth lives only in `src/app/admin/(protected)/layout.tsx`. Next renders layout and page in parallel, so the `redirect()` sets a 307 but the page body still streams. `curl -s /admin` with no cookie returned 307 plus the full RSC payload: every guest's name, email, country, "Chest 102 cm", "Bust 37 in", picks and notes. Browsers discard a 307 body; curl, a scraper, or a curious cousin with dev tools does not. `(protected)/page.tsx` loads `getAllGuests()` with no check. The export route does it right. Change: gate at the data or page level, not the layout.

**Biggest counter-risk.** Everything else is tolerable for ~100 guests on a link that is itself the secret. Over-engineering (accounts, OTP email, CAPTCHA) would hurt intake more than it protects: the realistic attacker is a leaked WhatsApp link, and the token-as-identity model already means "acting on another guest's token" is by design. The honest ranking is: one high, then a short tail of lows.

**First concrete step.** Add to `src/lib/adminAuth.ts` an exported `requireAdmin()` and call it as the first line of `(protected)/page.tsx`, `events/page.tsx`, `looks/page.tsx`, or better inside `getAllGuests()`/`getAllPlans()` in `src/lib/queries.ts` so no future page can forget. Re-run `curl -s /admin | grep -c example.com` and expect 0. Half a day including an e2e assertion.

**Other findings**
- Medium — `actions/admin.ts` login: no rate limit or delay; 25 wrong passcodes averaged 51 ms each with no lockout; add a 500 ms sleep on failure plus Vercel WAF rate-limiting.
- Medium — `adminAuth.ts` `isProduction()` requires `VERCEL`; on any other host with `ADMIN_PASSCODE` unset the passcode silently becomes `shanai-rhea` and `secure` is off; refuse to start without `ADMIN_PASSCODE` when `NODE_ENV=production`.
- Low — the cookie is a constant HMAC, identical for every login and never invalidated (curl still exported after logout); embed an issued-at timestamp.
- Low — `GuestForm.tsx` hint "allergies to fabrics" invites health data (GDPR Art. 9 / DPDP sensitive); reword to "fabrics you avoid".
- Low — no retention: state "deleted by 30 April 2027" on `/register` and honour it.
- Low — `actions/guest.ts` `lookIds` not checked against wardrobe/active/existence; a menswear guest stored a womenswear look; filter through `looksForEvent`.
- Low — `next.config.ts`: no `Referrer-Policy`/`X-Content-Type-Options`/CSP headers.
- Low — one-tap irreversible deletes with no confirm.

## Behavioural Designer / Conversion Specialist

**Take.** Intuitive: yes, for a guest who already has a tape measure in hand and ten free minutes. That guest does not exist on a WhatsApp link. The funnel is two pages and six required fields, which is lean, but it puts the one physical action (measuring) as a hard gate before anything is saved, then hands the guest a bookmark as the only way back. UI/UX: clean, honest, well-labelled, but built for a desk session, not for 40 seconds on a phone. In-page review of clothes: no; a guest picks from text cards and the "Browse at ↗" link ejects them into a store site with no route back. The couple will get a burst of registrations from the keen 20 percent and near-silence from the rest.

**Strongest argument.** The measurement wall plus the come-back problem, together. On `/register`, chest, waist, hip are required and validated positive; nothing is stored until they pass. A guest without a tape (most, on first open) cannot save anything, and there is no "I'll measure later" path. The only escape hatch, "Sizes you already know", sits below the required fields, is optional, and does not unlock the form. If they leave, nothing exists for them to return to. If they do finish, the welcome banner says "Bookmark this page… the only way back", offers no action (no copy, no WhatsApp share, no email), and vanishes the moment they save one event because it is keyed off `?welcome=1`. Email is required "so the couple can reach you" yet the spec forbids an email service, so the link is never sent anywhere. Change: let the guest save with known sizes only (measurements optional when `knownSizes` is filled, planner shows "add measurements for exact sizes"), and on first save push the private link back to them.

**Biggest counter-risk.** Making measurements optional trades completion for data quality: a planner full of "usually UK 12" rows gives the couple nothing the size engine can act on, and store reservations need real numbers. The counter is that a saved guest with a return link can be nudged to measure; an unsaved guest cannot be nudged at all. But if the couple's true bottleneck is reservation accuracy rather than headcount, keeping the gate and instead fixing the return path alone is the safer move.

**First concrete step.** In `src/app/me/[token]/page.tsx`, replace the `sp.welcome` banner with a persistent "Your private link" card containing a "Send to myself on WhatsApp" button (`https://wa.me/?text=<encoded URL>`) and a "Copy link" button, shown on every visit. Ships in a day, touches no schema or engine.

**Other findings**
- Default status is "Still deciding", so a guest can "Save Mehendi" and produce zero actionable signal; default to nothing selected and require a choice, or default to "Interested".
- Five separate forms with five Save buttons on a 6,400–8,900px page; each save reloads and shows a small "Saved" with no "2 of 5 done" progress, so guests stop after one.
- No deadline anywhere: every event reads "Date to be confirmed" and nothing says "reply by X for reservations", so there is no reason to do it today.
- Empty measurement fields return the raw zod string; write "Enter your chest in cm" and mention the tape.
- "Country you live in" looks required but validation accepts blank.
- The register submit button is 2.4 screens below the fold; required fields first, optional behind "More details".
- "Browse at Fabindia ↗" opens a store page in a new tab mid-form with unsaved ticks; either save on tick or warn.
- Womenswear guests get one size card (Fabindia only) while facing 3–6 look options per event from stores with no computed size, which undercuts the "we translate your measurements" promise.

## Round 2 — Interactive rebuttals (verbatim)

### Director of Product Design
**Updated take.** The planner's problem is not its height, it is that it asks guests to choose from things they cannot evaluate, and then asks the couple to act on choices no store can honour. My round-1 take treated the five-form page as a layout problem fixable with collapsibles; it now differs: the guest-side "tick a look" step should stop being the primary signal, and the page should be rebuilt around one intent, one budget, one save, with the cards demoted to reference.

**Where you concede.** The Destination Wedding Planner moved me most: "Please reserve" saved with no look selected, and even with one, the look is a category so nothing can be held against it (`savePlan` never relates `lookIds` to `status`). That makes my round-1 "compact chooser" a nicer version of a broken contract. The Behavioural Designer is right that the return path is worse than I said: the banner is keyed off `?welcome=1` so it vanishes after the first event save, and the "Saved" text only renders when `sp.saved` matches, 1,300 px below the hash target; guests get neither a way back nor a confirmation. The Security Engineer's verified 307-body leak outranks every design change on my list.

**Where you sharpen disagreement.** Head of Merchandising's "per-event, per-price-tier, colour-aware curation" as text is the wrong investment. I also disagree with the Frontend Engineer's ranking of the a11y fixes first: they are an afternoon and belong in the first bundle, but a keyboard guest can still register; a guest who lost the link cannot come back. And I part from the Behavioural Designer on fully optional measurements: "usually UK 12" gives the size engine, the site's one real asset, nothing to compute.

**Positions on A, B, C.**

Improving text cards fails for the reasons above. Couple-uploaded photos need a storage capability, in-store photography of ~15 looks, and moderation, for maybe 60 guests; and a photo of a sherwani still cannot be reserved by category. Dropping guest choosing is worse only if guests are expected to shop themselves, and the site's status list already says otherwise.

## Recommendation

Make the couple the buyer: the guest's job becomes measurements, one intent, a budget band, and where they will be; the look cards stay as reference, without a checkbox that pretends to reserve anything. Before any guest link goes out, close the admin data leak.

The Security Engineer's and Wedding Planner's concerns dominate, and they point the same way. The leak is irreversible harm once a link circulates, and every other proposal on the table (reservation view, budget, dependants) adds more data to the same unguarded surface, so it is first by a wide margin. The Planner's finding that "Please reserve" can be saved with nothing to reserve, and that even a chosen look is a store department, is the structural flaw behind the owner's third question: no, a guest cannot review clothes on this page, and the panel's judgement is that they should not have to. Store pages are client-rendered, neither store ships abroad, and photos would pin out-of-stock SKUs and create an upload chore for the couple. The size engine plus an intent plus a budget band is the row the couple can walk into Tasva Lower Parel with.

The Design Director and Behavioural Designer moved most between rounds. Both started with layout and completion remedies (collapsibles, optional measurements) and ended by conceding that the guest-side chooser is the wrong contract, which is where the real insight sits: shorten the page by removing the decision, not by folding it.

Two dissents are accepted with mitigations. The Merchandiser argues Indian occasion wear is chosen by garment, colour, fabric and price tier without a photo, and that evening wear is where a shortlist matters most. That is right as content and wrong as a gate: keep the cards, add colour, fabric and "what it needs" as editable fields later, but do not make the reserve status depend on a tick. The Frontend Engineer keeps the tick as "I'd like to see this"; that is compatible with the recommendation if `lookIds` stays optional, and it costs nothing, so it survives as a preference signal rather than an order.

On the measurement wall the panel converged on keep, then move: measurements stay required for the size engine to be worth anything, and the two-step register (record and link first, tape second) is the mitigation, deferred until there is a denominator that shows drop-off. Budget is a band, not a number, and never worded as authorisation, because the link is shareable by design.
